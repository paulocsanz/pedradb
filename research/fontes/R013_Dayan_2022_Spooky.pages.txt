ABSTRACT
Modern storage engines and key-value stores have come to rely on
the log-structured merge-tree (LSM-tree) as their core data struc-
ture. LSM-tree operates by gradually merge-sorting data across
levels of exponentially increasing capacities in storage. A crucial
design dimension of LSM-tree is its compaction granularity. Some
designs perform Full Merge, whereby entire levels get compacted
at once. Others perform Partial Merge, whereby smaller groups of
files with overlapping key ranges are compacted independently.
This paper shows that both strategies exhibit serious flaws. With
Full Merge, space-amplification is exorbitant. The reason is that                             Figure 1: Existing LSM-trees cannot reach high storage uti-
